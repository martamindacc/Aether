## Why a weekend can do what weekly often can't

Fifty minutes a week has a structural problem: the first fifteen go to catching up, the last ten go to landing the plane, and the middle twenty-five is where the work happens, if nobody cancelled. Then a week passes, the pattern reasserts itself at home, and next session starts by re-finding the thread.

An intensive removes the gaps. What surfaces at 10am is worked at 2pm. What one partner finally says on day one is still in the room on day two. The couple doesn't get to retreat between sessions, and neither does the therapist. For many couples, this is the first time they have stayed in a hard conversation long enough to get through it.

## Who intensives are for

**Couples in crisis.** A disclosed affair, a stated intention to leave, a conflict that has escalated past what weekly can hold. When a month of waiting would do more damage, an intensive stabilises first and repairs second. [Couples intensives for crisis →](/blog/couples-intensive-crisis-infidelity-separation)

**Couples whose calendars defeat weekly therapy.** Founders, executives, people who travel, couples living between cities or countries. A weekend you actually protect beats fifteen Tuesdays you keep moving. [Intensives for executives and founders →](/blog/couples-intensive-executives-founders)

**Couples who have stalled in weekly therapy.** You've done the work, you understand the pattern, and nothing shifts. Sustained time without escape routes is often what breaks through.

**Couples who want to do it properly, once.** Who prefer concentrated, serious work over months of incremental steps.

**Couples preparing for something.** A move, a child, a company sale, a career change. One or two days to reinforce the relationship before the pressure arrives.

**Couples who want to decide.** Stay or go, with clarity. A discernment-focused intensive gives both partners room to say what they actually think.

## Who intensives are not for

An intensive is demanding and it requires both partners to be in the room, in every sense. It is not the right first step when one partner is unwilling, when there is violence, threat, coercion or active substance misuse in the relationship, when one partner is in acute psychiatric crisis, or when an affair is still ongoing. In those situations we'll say so in the consultation and point you to what fits. [Intensive vs weekly: which is right for you →](/blog/intensive-vs-weekly-couples-therapy)

## Formats

| Format | Length | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **One-day intensive** | 6–7 hours | Prevention, premarital work, a specific stuck issue, booster for returning couples |
| **Two-day intensive** | 12–14 hours over consecutive days | The standard format: pattern work, repair, reconnection |
| **Three-day intensive** | 16–20 hours | Infidelity recovery, long-entrenched patterns, discernment with high stakes |
| **Split intensive** | Two one-day blocks, 2–4 weeks apart | Couples who can't do consecutive days, or who benefit from integration time |

All formats include a pre-intensive consultation, individual preparation for each partner, and two to three follow-up sessions in the weeks after. [What happens in a two-day intensive, hour by hour →](/blog/two-day-couples-intensive-hour-by-hour)

## How we work

Our intensives are built on Emotionally Focused Therapy, with structured elements drawn from the Gottman approach where they fit. The arc over two days moves from mapping the negative cycle you're caught in, through the harder work of what each of you is protecting beneath it, to practising new ways of reaching each other with the therapist in the room, then agreeing what you take home.

Sessions run in 90-minute blocks with real breaks. Evenings are for rest, not homework. Follow-ups anchor the change in ordinary life, which is where it has to survive.

We work in English and Norwegian, online, across US and European time zones.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do we book an intensive?

Start with the free 15-minute consultation. We'll assess fit, recommend a format, and if you proceed, send each of you a short preparation questionnaire. Intensive dates are typically available within two to four weeks; sooner for crisis.

### Can we do an intensive online?

Yes. Online intensives follow the same structure with the same follow-ups. You need a private space where you can both speak freely for the full day. Being in the same room as each other, with the therapist on screen, is part of the setup; we'll go through the practical details in the consultation.

### Do we need to be in Oslo or the US?

No. Intensives are online and available wherever you are, across US and European time zones. We work in English and Norwegian.

### What if we need to cancel or reschedule?

Intensive dates are held exclusively for you, so we ask for notice and apply a rescheduling policy that we'll share at booking. Crisis situations are handled case by case.

### Will we need therapy afterwards?

Follow-up sessions are included because they are where the change gets anchored. After that, some couples continue with occasional sessions, some return for a booster day before a known pressure point, and many don't need more. We agree the plan together on day two.

### Is an intensive more expensive than weekly therapy?

Per package, yes; in total, often no. A two-day intensive typically replaces three to six months of weekly sessions, and couples who commit to a weekend complete the work at far higher rates than couples in weekly therapy. The full comparison is in [Intensive vs weekly](/blog/intensive-vs-weekly-couples-therapy).

### Can we combine an intensive with weekly therapy?

Yes, and many couples do: an intensive to break through, then weekly or fortnightly sessions to consolidate. Or weekly therapy with a booster intensive when progress stalls. We'll recommend what fits.
