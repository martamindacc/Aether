## If a deadlock turns legal in New York

Most founding teams never take a deadlock to court. If yours is heading there, these are the two rules New York founders most often meet.

- **A New York corporation.** Under [Business Corporation Law § 1104](https://newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._business_corporation_law_section_1104), a holder of half the voting shares can petition a court to dissolve the company on grounds of deadlock. Courts treat it as a drastic remedy: a business that is still operating can survive the petition, and a tie-break clause in the shareholders' agreement can defeat it.
- **A Delaware corporation**, as many venture-backed New York startups are. [Section 273](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title8/c001/sc10/index.shtml) of the Delaware General Corporation Law covers only a narrow case: two stockholders owning half each in a joint venture who cannot agree whether to end it.

Both routes are slow, public and blunt. They settle who keeps the company, not how the two of you work together. This is general information, not legal advice: for your own situation, talk to New York counsel.
