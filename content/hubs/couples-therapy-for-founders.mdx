## The relationship is the one thing in your life without a board

You have investors who ask hard questions. A team that depends on you. Metrics that tell you, weekly, whether things are working. Everything in your professional life has accountability built in.

Your relationship has none of that. Nobody sends a reminder when it's been three months since you had a real conversation. There's no dashboard for how lonely your partner has become. No board meeting where someone asks whether the two of you are still on the same side.

So the relationship is where the cuts land. Not because you decided that. Because it's the only thing that doesn't push back until it's already in trouble.

Couples therapy for founders exists to put that accountability back, before the damage is structural.

## Who this is for

**Founder + partner couples.** One of you is building a company. The other is living with the company as a third presence in the relationship: the risk, the hours, the moods after a bad board meeting, the question of when, if ever, it ends. The non-founder partner is often the most under-served person in the startup ecosystem. [We write specifically for them.](/blog/when-your-partner-is-the-founder)

**Married or partnered co-founders.** You run a company together and share a life together. The board meeting bleeds into dinner. A disagreement about hiring is also a disagreement between spouses. There is nowhere to go. [Read: Co-founder couples: when your business partner is your spouse.](/blog/co-founder-couples-married)

**Couples in a fundraising season.** The months where everything is on the line and the founder is unreachable even when present. [Read: Fundraising season and your relationship.](/blog/fundraising-and-your-relationship)

**Couples after an exit or liquidity event.** The company sold, the money is real, and somehow the relationship is harder, not easier. Identity, purpose, money, and who you are to each other now. [Read: The relationship after the exit.](/blog/relationship-after-the-exit)

**Couples who look fine from the outside.** Successful, functional, admired. And distant. You don't fight much. You don't talk much either. [Read: Signs a founder marriage is in trouble.](/blog/signs-founder-marriage-in-trouble)

## Why standard couples therapy often misses founders

The evidence-based methods, Emotionally Focused Therapy, the Gottman approach, systemic therapy, work. The research is solid. But they were developed and validated with couples who had regular hours, roughly symmetrical power, and identities that weren't fused to a company's survival. Applied without adaptation, three things go wrong:

**The pace is wrong.** You don't have six sessions to build rapport before touching the problem. You need a therapist who identifies the pattern in the first or second hour and gives you something usable immediately.

**The context is missing.** When your partner says "the bridge round fell through and he hasn't looked at me since," the therapist needs to know what a bridge round is and what it does to a person. Not to take the founder's side. To understand what's load-bearing. You should not have to explain your world from scratch.

**The vulnerability model is wrong.** Founders have been rewarded their entire careers for projecting certainty. Asking them to be emotionally exposed in a room with a stranger, after a day of back-to-back high-stakes decisions, requires a different approach to trust than the standard model assumes.

Couples therapy for founders keeps the evidence base and changes the delivery.

## What we work on

**The pattern under the conflict.** You're almost never fighting about what you think you're fighting about. The argument about dinner is about presence. The argument about money is about risk one of you chose and the other lives with. We find the pattern fast and name it, so it becomes something you fight together rather than something you fight about.

**The business as the third person.** Where the company ends and the relationship begins. For founder-partner couples, that means giving the partner a real voice in a risk they didn't choose. For co-founder couples, it means two distinct modes of conversation with their own rules.

**Asymmetry.** One of you is consumed, the other carries everything else. One gets the recognition, the other gets the invisible load. One has a world outside, the other has mostly you. Asymmetry isn't a character flaw. It is a structural feature of founder relationships, and it has to be named before it becomes resentment.

**Presence, not scheduling.** You've tried calendaring date nights. The gap didn't close, because the gap isn't a scheduling problem. It's a depth-of-presence problem. We work on what happens inside the time you do have.

**Money and values.** Especially after liquidity. Money that was theoretical becomes real, and suddenly you discover you've never agreed on what it's for. [Read: Money and values after a liquidity event.](/blog/money-and-values-after-liquidity)

**Intimacy.** Desire doesn't survive chronic stress unexamined. We talk about it directly, without awkwardness, because it is usually a symptom of the pattern rather than a separate problem. [Read: Startup stress and intimacy.](/blog/startup-stress-and-intimacy)

## How it works

**Free 15-minute consultation.** You tell us where you are. We tell you honestly whether we're the right fit, and if not, who might be.

**Discovery session (60–90 min).** Both of you, together. How you met, what changed, what each of you sees as the problem (you don't have to agree), what the company has done to your life. We identify the main pattern and propose a direction.

**Maintenance.** Many founder couples keep a quarterly session, or return before a known pressure point: a raise, a launch, a sale.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is couples therapy for founders?

Couples therapy for founders is evidence-based relationship therapy adapted to the specific pressures of building a company: long hours, financial risk, identity fused with the venture, and a partner who lives with all of it. It is faster-paced and more direct than standard couples therapy, and assumes the therapist understands the startup context.

### Is this for co-founders who aren't romantic partners?

This hub is for couples: romantic partners where one or both are founders. Non-romantic co-founders increasingly use couples-therapy methods too, and we can discuss that in a consultation, but the articles here are written for couples who share a life, not only a company.

### How is it different from executive coaching or co-founder coaching?

Coaching focuses on performance and the business relationship. Couples therapy focuses on the emotional bond between you, the patterns that have formed, and what each of you needs. Many founder couples benefit from both, but they are not the same thing.

### Do you take insurance?

We are private-pay. US clients can request a superbill for out-of-network reimbursement if their plan covers it. Norwegian clients pay directly; couples therapy is not reimbursed by HELFO.

### Can we do this online?

Yes. Most founder couples do. We work across US and European time zones, which suits couples who travel or live between places. Intensive formats are online too.

### What if only one of us wants to come?

Common, and workable. The founder is often the reluctant one. You can start alone, and in our experience the other partner frequently joins once something starts shifting.

### How long does it take?

Varies. Some couples see meaningful change in 4–6 sessions; others work for several months. Intensives compress the early phase into two days. We agree on goals and a review point early, so it never drifts.
