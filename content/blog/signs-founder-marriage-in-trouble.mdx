Nobody in the startup world will tell you your marriage is in trouble. Your co-founder won't; it's not their place. Your investors won't; they care about the company. Your founder friends won't; theirs look the same. And your partner may have stopped telling you, because they've learned it doesn't land.

So the signs accumulate in the one place with no accountability, until something happens that forces them into view: an exit, an affair, a quiet "I think we should talk," a child who asks why you're never home.

This article is the warning you weren't going to get otherwise.

## Short answer: How do I know if the company is hurting my marriage?

Not by whether you fight. Founder marriages often don't. By whether you've stopped reaching for each other.

The reliable indicators are absence of positive contact rather than presence of conflict: conversations that are only logistical, a partner who no longer asks about your day or shares theirs, intimacy that has quietly stopped, decisions made alone, and a deferral of the relationship to an "after" that keeps moving. Each of these can be explained by the company. Together, they're a pattern. And the pattern, not the company, is what ends marriages.

## Why founder couples miss the signs

Three reasons, all structural.

**Every sign has an innocent explanation.** Absence: the raise. Flatness: exhaustion. No sex: stress. Each is true. The problem is treating each in isolation and never asking what they add up to.

**The founder has mistaken steadiness for wellbeing.** The partner has held everything together so well that it looks like they're fine. Over-functioning is invisible from the outside, including to the person benefiting from it.

**Both of you are waiting for "after."** Which means both of you have agreed, implicitly, not to assess the relationship in its current state. The assessment is deferred along with everything else.

## The nine signs

### 1. Your conversations are entirely logistical

*What it looks like:* Who's picking up the kids. What's happening this weekend. Whether the thing got paid. Efficient, functional, cooperative.

*Why it reads as normal:* You're a good team. You're getting things done under pressure. That's what founder couples do.

*What it means:* The intimate conversation, how are you, what are you afraid of, what do you need from me, has stopped. Logistics is what's left when connection has gone. Teams don't need to feel close to function. Marriages do.

### 2. Your partner has stopped asking about the company

*What it looks like:* They used to ask how it was going. Now they don't. You might even find it a relief.

*Why it reads as normal:* They've learned the answer is either "fine" or a monologue. They're giving you space.

*What it means:* They've given up trying to get in. Not asking is self-protection after repeated small rejections. The relief you feel is the sound of a door closing.

### 3. Your partner has stopped telling you about their life

*What it looks like:* You realise you don't know what's going on with their work, their friend, their health. They haven't mentioned it. You haven't asked.

*Why it reads as normal:* They know you're at capacity. They're being considerate.

*What it means:* They've concluded there's no room for them. Their inner life has moved somewhere else: friends, work, or nowhere. This is the sign that most often precedes a partner saying, apparently out of nowhere, that they're done.

### 4. "After" has moved more than twice

*What it looks like:* After the seed round. After the launch. After the Series A. After we hire a COO. After the next raise.

*Why it reads as normal:* Startups have phases. The next one really is critical.

*What it means:* The relationship has no claim on the present. Partners stop believing in "after" around the second or third move, and when they stop believing, they stop waiting. That can look like independence or like withdrawal. Either way, you've lost the thread.

### 5. Intimacy has stopped and nobody has mentioned it

*What it looks like:* Sex is rare or absent. Touch has reduced too. It isn't discussed.

*Why it reads as normal:* Stress. Exhaustion. Kids. Everyone's sex life drops during a build.

*What it means:* The stress is real, but the silence is the sign. Couples who are connected talk about the dry spell. Couples who have drifted don't, because talking would require closeness that isn't there. [Read: Startup stress and intimacy.](/blog/startup-stress-and-intimacy)

### 6. Decisions that affect both of you are being made by one of you

*What it looks like:* A relocation, a personal guarantee, taking less salary, a hire that changes the founder's hours, bringing on an investor with strings. The partner finds out after, or is informed rather than consulted.

*Why it reads as normal:* The founder decides fast; that's the job. The partner "wouldn't understand the details anyway."

*What it means:* The partner has become a stakeholder, not a partner. Stakeholders get informed. Partners decide together. The shift from one to the other is rarely announced and almost always felt.

### 7. The partner has built a life that doesn't require you

*What it looks like:* Their own friends, their own weekends, their own plans. They don't seem to mind your absence anymore.

*Why it reads as normal:* Healthy independence. You're glad they're not sitting at home waiting.

*What it means:* Sometimes it is healthy. Often it's the final stage of giving up: they've stopped expecting you to show up, and arranged their life accordingly. The tell is whether they still want you in it when you're available. If they've stopped inviting you, pay attention.

### 8. You feel more alone at home than at work

*What it looks like:* Work is stressful but you're surrounded by people who get it. Home is quiet, and the quiet feels cold rather than restful.

*Why it reads as normal:* Work is exciting. Home is where you crash.

*What it means:* Home has stopped being a refuge. For founders, who are isolated professionally almost by definition, losing home as the place of connection is losing the last one. [Read: Founder loneliness, from the partner's side.](/blog/founder-loneliness-partner-side)

### 9. One of you has had the thought "maybe after the exit we'll separate"

*What it looks like:* A passing thought. Not a plan. Dismissed quickly.

*Why it reads as normal:* Everyone has dark thoughts under pressure.

*What it means:* A relationship that is being consciously held together until a liquidity event is already, in an important sense, over. The thought is information. It deserves a conversation, not dismissal.

## Self-check: Score yourselves

Each of you, separately. One point per yes.

**0–2:** The company is costing you, as it costs everyone. Stay alert.
**3–5:** A pattern has formed. It will not reverse on its own, and it is very recoverable with deliberate effort.
**6–9:** The marriage is being held together by structure, not connection. Act now, not after.

If your scores differ by three or more, that gap is itself the most important finding. One of you is seeing something the other isn't.

## What the signs don't mean

They don't mean the founder doesn't love their partner. Most founders in failing marriages love their partners and are shocked to discover the state of things.

They don't mean the partner has checked out. Most partners who've stopped asking would start again instantly if asking worked.

They don't mean the company has to go. Plenty of founder marriages recover while the company continues. The company isn't the problem. The pattern is.

They don't mean it's too late. Couples recover from six or seven of these signs regularly. What they can't recover from is continuing not to look.

## "It'll be better after": the sign behind the signs

If there is one belief that produces all nine, it's this one.

"After" is the founder's promise and the partner's hope. It is also the mechanism by which the relationship is removed from the present and placed in a future that never arrives. As long as both of you believe in after, neither of you has to look at now.

The couples who recover are the ones who stop believing in after, together, and ask: what does the relationship get *now*, in the current conditions, with the company exactly as demanding as it is? The answer is usually small. Ten minutes a day. One protected hour a week. One real question. But it's now, and now is the only place a marriage can actually live.

## What to do if you recognise three or more

**Say it.** Tonight. Not "we need to talk." Something like: "I read something that described us, and it scared me. I don't want to lose us to this. Can we look at it together?"

**Stop waiting for after.** Agree one thing that happens now, regardless of the company. Small and inviolable.

**Change one conversation a day.** Ten minutes that aren't logistics. "How are you, actually." Then listen without solving.

**Founder: ask what your partner has stopped telling you.** And brace for the answer.

**Partner: say the thing you've been saving.** Early, plainly, without apology.

**Get help early.** Three signs is the right time for a therapist. Not seven. Founder couples wait until seven because asking for help feels like admitting failure. It isn't. It's what high performers do with every other problem they can't solve alone.

## What if only one of you sees it?

Common, and usually it's the partner who sees it and the founder who doesn't.

If you're the partner: say it anyway, specifically. Not "you're never here" but "I've stopped telling you things, and I noticed that, and it frightens me." Then, if the founder can't hear it, go to therapy alone. Partners who start alone frequently shift things enough that the founder follows.

If you're the founder and your partner has said something like this: believe them. They have more information than you do. You've been in the building. They've been in the marriage.

## When it's more than trouble

Some things are not startup costs and should not be absorbed as such:

- Contempt: speaking to each other with disdain, mockery, or as a problem to be managed
- Deception about money or risk that binds both of you
- An affair, emotional or physical
- One partner's health, mental or physical, deteriorating without acknowledgement
- Any form of control, intimidation or threat, which is a safety issue, not a relationship one

If these are present, the question is no longer whether the company is hurting the marriage. It is what the marriage is, and that needs direct attention.

## FAQ: Signs a founder marriage is in trouble

### What are the signs a startup is hurting your marriage?

Not conflict, usually. Absence of contact: conversations that are only logistical, a partner who has stopped asking about the company and stopped sharing their own life, intimacy that has stopped undiscussed, decisions made by one partner alone, a partner who has built a life that no longer needs you, feeling more alone at home than at work, and "after" having moved more than twice.

### Is it normal for founder marriages to go through a hard phase?

Yes, and the phase is survivable if it's seen as a phase. The danger is when the hard phase becomes the pattern and nobody notices because every sign has a reasonable startup explanation. Couples who name it while it's happening recover. Couples who defer the assessment to "after" often find there's little left.

### Why do founders miss the signs their marriage is in trouble?

Because each sign has an innocent explanation (the raise, exhaustion, stress), because the partner's over-functioning makes them look fine, and because both have agreed implicitly to defer the relationship to "after," which means never assessing it now. Founders are also often the last to know, because the partner has stopped saying what isn't landing.

### How many signs mean the marriage is in serious trouble?

Three or more indicates a pattern that won't reverse on its own but is very recoverable with deliberate effort. Six or more indicates the marriage is being held together by structure rather than connection, and needs action now. A gap of three or more between partners' scores is itself a serious finding: one of you is seeing something the other isn't.

### Can a founder marriage recover from these signs?

Yes, including from six or seven of them, and without the founder leaving the company. What marriages don't recover from is continuing not to look. The pattern, not the company, is what ends them, and patterns can be changed. Earlier is faster; three signs is the right time to get help, not seven.

### What's the single most important thing to change?

Stop deferring the relationship to "after." Agree what the marriage gets now, in the current conditions, with the company exactly as demanding as it is. Usually small: ten minutes a day, one protected hour a week, one real question. But now is the only place a marriage can actually live.

### What if my founder partner doesn't see the problem?

Say it specifically and without accusation: "I've stopped telling you things, and that frightens me." If they can't hear it, start therapy alone. Partners who go first frequently shift the dynamic enough that the founder follows. And founders: if your partner says this, believe them. They've been in the marriage; you've been in the building.
