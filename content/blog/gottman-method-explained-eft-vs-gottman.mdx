If you've looked into couples therapy, you've met the name. Gottman shows up in therapist bios, in relationship books, in the "four horsemen" articles that circulate every time someone writes about marriage. It's the most recognisable brand in couples work, and the recognition is earned: the underlying research is some of the most influential ever done on relationships.

It's also widely misunderstood, often by the people citing it. This is what it actually is.

## Short answer: What is the Gottman Method?

The Gottman Method is a couples therapy approach developed by John and Julie Gottman, grounded in longitudinal observational research on couples. Its core claim is that relationship success is predicted by observable patterns of interaction, particularly how couples handle conflict and how much positive connection they maintain outside it.

In practice, it's structured and skills-based: a formal assessment of the relationship, a framework (the Sound Relationship House) that maps what a strong relationship requires, and specific interventions aimed at reducing destructive conflict behaviours, increasing fondness and "turning toward," and building shared purpose.

It is one of the two most widely practised couples approaches in the English-speaking world. The other is Emotionally Focused Therapy, and the two differ in important ways covered below.

## Where it came from: the research

The Gottman approach is unusual among therapy models in that it started as research, not as a clinical method.

From the 1970s, John Gottman and colleagues observed couples in laboratory settings, recording conversations about conflict and measuring behaviour, physiology and later self-report. Over years, they tracked which couples stayed together and which separated, and looked for patterns in the early recordings that distinguished them.

Several findings became famous:

- A small set of conflict behaviours (the "four horsemen") was strongly associated with later separation.
- The ratio of positive to negative interactions during conflict mattered; stable couples maintained a markedly positive ratio even while disagreeing.
- Couples who responded to each other's small bids for attention, "turning toward" rather than away, were far more likely to stay together.
- Physiological flooding during conflict, measurable heart-rate elevation, predicted withdrawal and poor repair.
- Most relationship conflicts were about perpetual differences, not solvable problems, and stable couples managed those differences rather than resolving them.

The therapy was then built to target the things the research said mattered. That's a reasonable design logic, and it's why Gottman-based work feels so concrete: it's aimed at observed behaviours.

## The Sound Relationship House

The organising framework is a house with seven levels and two walls. In plain terms:

**Levels (bottom to top):**

1. **Knowing each other's inner world.** Keeping an up-to-date map of your partner's stresses, hopes, history and preferences. Not the facts of their life; the texture of it.
2. **Fondness and admiration.** Actively noticing and expressing what you value about each other. The habit of appreciation rather than scanning for what's wrong.
3. **Turning toward.** Responding to the small bids for connection that happen dozens of times a day: a comment, a question, a sigh. Each response builds or spends connection.
4. **The positive perspective.** When the lower levels are solid, partners interpret each other charitably. When they're not, neutral acts get read as hostile.
5. **Managing conflict.** Distinguishing solvable problems from perpetual ones; soft start-ups; accepting influence; repair during conflict; physiological self-soothing.
6. **Making life dreams come true.** Understanding and supporting what each partner wants their life to be.
7. **Shared meaning.** Rituals, roles, goals and symbols the couple builds together.

**Walls:** trust and commitment, which hold the house up.

The model's practical implication: conflict skills (level 5) sit on top of friendship (levels 1–4). Couples who try to fix how they fight without rebuilding the friendship underneath tend to fail, because the charitable interpretation that makes repair possible isn't there.

## The Four Horsemen, briefly

The most famous Gottman concept: four conflict behaviours associated with relationship failure, each with a recommended antidote. In outline:

- **Criticism** (attacking character rather than raising a specific complaint) → **gentle start-up**: state the specific issue and your need.
- **Contempt** (disrespect, mockery, superiority) → **a culture of appreciation**: actively building fondness so contempt has no ground.
- **Defensiveness** (counter-attacking or playing victim instead of hearing the complaint) → **taking responsibility** for your part, even if small.
- **Stonewalling** (shutting down and withdrawing) → **physiological self-soothing**: a structured break, then return.

Contempt is identified in the research as the single strongest predictor of separation.

We cover each horseman, its antidote and a self-check in depth in [The Four Horsemen: how to recognise them and the antidotes](/blog/four-horsemen-gottman-antidotes). This article stays at the method level.

## What a Gottman-style therapy course looks like

Gottman Method therapy is more structured than most, and couples who like structure often prefer it for that reason.

**Assessment phase (typically 2–3 sessions).** A joint session covering the relationship's history and current concerns; individual sessions with each partner; and standardised questionnaires covering the levels of the house, conflict patterns, and risk factors. The therapist then presents a formal assessment to the couple: where the relationship is strong, where it's vulnerable, what the work will target.

**Treatment phase.** Sessions are interventional and often exercise-based. Depending on the assessment, the therapist might work on:
- Rebuilding knowledge of each other's inner world through structured conversation
- Building appreciation habits
- Identifying and practising responses to bids
- Teaching soft start-up, repair attempts and accepting influence, often by having the couple discuss a real issue in session with the therapist coaching in real time
- Mapping perpetual problems and finding what's underneath each partner's position (the "dream within the conflict")
- Creating rituals of connection and shared meaning

**Format.** Weekly or fortnightly sessions are standard. The Gottmans also developed an intensive "marathon" format of consecutive full days, which has influenced intensive couples therapy generally. [See: Intensive couples therapy.](/intensive-couples-therapy)

**Homework.** Gottman-based work typically assigns exercises between sessions. Couples who do them tend to progress; couples who don't, stall.

## What the research actually supports, and what it doesn't

This is where most Gottman articles, including many written by Gottman-trained therapists, are less than straight. The honest picture has two parts.

**The observational research is strong and influential.** The findings about contempt, turning toward, flooding, perpetual problems and positive-to-negative ratios are well replicated in spirit across the relationship-science field, and they've shaped how almost every couples therapist, of any orientation, understands conflict. This is a genuine contribution.

**The famous "predicts divorce with 90%+ accuracy" claims have been questioned.** Independent researchers pointed out in the early 2000s that the prediction models were built and tested on the same data, which inflates apparent accuracy; cross-validation on new samples produced much lower figures. This doesn't undermine the findings about what matters in relationships. It does mean the headline accuracy numbers you'll see repeated in articles should be treated cautiously.

**The clinical-trial evidence for Gottman Method therapy as a treatment is thinner than its reputation suggests.** There are supportive studies, including randomised trials of Gottman-based workshops and programmes showing improvements in satisfaction and conflict, and clinician-reported outcomes for the therapy. But the body of controlled trials testing Gottman Method Couples Therapy as delivered, against comparison conditions, with follow-up, is smaller than for Emotionally Focused Therapy or for behavioural couple therapies. The approach's authority rests more on the research that informed it than on trials of the therapy itself.

**The practical reading.** The Gottman Method is a well-constructed, research-informed approach that works for many couples, and nothing in the literature suggests it's ineffective. But if you're choosing on evidence base alone, EFT currently has the stronger clinical-trial support. Most experienced couples therapists, including ones certified in one model, would acknowledge this privately.

## Who the Gottman Method suits

- **Couples who want structure and tools.** If you'd rather have a framework, a diagnosis of where the relationship is weak, and concrete exercises, Gottman-based work delivers that directly.
- **Couples whose main problem is how they fight.** Escalating conflict, criticism and contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling. The method targets these specifically.
- **Couples who like to understand the "why."** The research grounding is explicit, and many couples find it motivating to know that what they're practising has an evidence rationale.
- **Couples who will do homework.** The method depends on practice between sessions.
- **Couples whose friendship has eroded but whose attachment is intact.** Rebuilding the lower levels of the house is something the method does well.
- **High-functioning couples who want to prevent problems.** The structured, educational quality suits couples who aren't in crisis but want to invest.

## Who it suits less well

- **Couples whose core problem is emotional distance rather than conflict.** Where one partner has withdrawn and the other has given up, skills training can feel like it's addressing the wrong layer. The problem isn't how they argue; it's that one of them has stopped reaching. EFT tends to serve these couples better.
- **Couples where the "skills" become another performance.** High achievers in particular can execute soft start-ups and repair attempts flawlessly while nothing underneath changes. [We write about this problem elsewhere.](/guides/high-achievers-guide-relationship-career)
- **Couples who experience structure as mechanical.** Some couples find the assessment-and-exercise rhythm clinical and distancing.
- **Couples dealing with an attachment injury.** A betrayal, an abandonment at a critical moment. Gottman work addresses trust rebuilding, but EFT's specific model for attachment injury repair is more developed.
- **Couples who won't do homework.** The method stalls without it.

## Gottman vs EFT: the honest comparison

Nearly every comparison online is written by someone certified in one or the other. We're EFT-based, so read the following with that known, and note that we're trying to be fair to both.

| | Gottman Method | Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT) |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Observational research on what predicts stability, then therapy built to target it | Attachment theory and experiential therapy, then clinical trials |
| **Core idea** | Relationship success is driven by observable interaction patterns: conflict behaviours, turning toward, friendship | Relationship distress is driven by insecure attachment: a negative cycle protecting unmet needs for safety and connection |
| **What it works on** | Skills and habits: how you fight, how you connect, shared meaning | The cycle between you and the emotions underneath it; reaching each other differently |
| **Style** | Structured, assessment-led, exercise-based, educational | Process-led, emotion-focused, therapist tracks the cycle in the room |
| **Homework** | Central | Minimal; change happens mostly in session |
| **Strongest evidence** | The underlying observational research | Randomised clinical trials of the therapy, with long-term follow-up |
| **Best for** | Conflict-heavy couples; couples wanting tools; prevention | Distance-and-withdrawal couples; attachment injuries; couples where "we don't fight, we just don't connect" |
| **Risk** | Can feel mechanical; skills can be performed without change | Can feel slow or abstract to couples who want concrete tools; requires emotional access |
| **Typical length** | 10–20 sessions plus homework | 8–20 sessions |
| **Intensive format** | Yes ("marathon" format pioneered here) | Yes, widely offered |

**Which fits you?**

If your fights are loud, frequent and ugly, and you want a toolkit and a framework, Gottman-based work is a strong fit.

If your relationship is quiet, distant, and one or both of you has stopped reaching, EFT is usually the better fit.

If you've been betrayed and need to rebuild trust after an attachment injury, EFT's specific model for that is a strength.

If you want the approach with the strongest clinical-trial evidence, that's EFT.

If you're a high achiever who's likely to perform the exercises perfectly without anything shifting, be cautious with pure skills-based work, and look for a therapist (of either orientation) who knows how to work past the performance.

If you want prevention and you're not in distress, Gottman-based educational work is excellent.

Most couples don't fit cleanly into one box, which is why most experienced therapists draw on both.

## Can a therapist use both?

Yes, and most experienced couples therapists do, whatever their primary training.

The models are compatible at the level of practice. An EFT therapist will readily use Gottman-derived ideas about bids, turning toward, and the horsemen as ways of describing what's happening between partners. A Gottman-trained therapist will work with the emotion underneath a perpetual problem in ways that look very like EFT.

Where they differ is the primary lens: whether the therapist's organising question is "what behaviours need to change" or "what is this cycle protecting." A good therapist can tell you which lens is primary for them and why, and can explain how they'd adapt for you.

## How to find a Gottman-trained therapist

If, after all this, Gottman-based work is what you want:

- **Certified Gottman Therapists** have completed the full training sequence including supervised practice. The Gottman Institute maintains a referral directory.
- **Gottman Levels 1–3 trained** indicates completion of the training workshops without full certification; still meaningful, but ask how much supervised couples work they've done.
- **"Gottman-informed"** means the therapist uses ideas from the approach. Fine, and common, but not the same as training in the method.

Ask the same questions you'd ask any couples therapist: what proportion of their caseload is couples, how they hold both partners, what the first sessions look like. [Our full guide to choosing a couples therapist is here.](/guides/how-to-choose-couples-therapist-founders-executives)

## FAQ: The Gottman Method

### What is the Gottman Method in simple terms?

A structured couples therapy approach based on research into what distinguishes couples who stay together from couples who separate. It assesses the relationship, maps it against a model called the Sound Relationship House, and teaches specific skills for handling conflict, maintaining friendship and building shared meaning. It's concrete, exercise-based and research-grounded.

### Is the Gottman Method evidence-based?

Partly. The observational research underlying it, on contempt, turning toward, flooding and perpetual problems, is strong and influential. The clinical-trial evidence for the therapy itself is thinner than its reputation suggests, with fewer controlled trials than Emotionally Focused Therapy. The famous divorce-prediction accuracy figures have been questioned by independent researchers. The method is well-supported as an approach; it's not the most trial-tested one.

### What is the Sound Relationship House?

The Gottman model of what a strong relationship requires: seven levels (knowing each other's inner world, fondness and admiration, turning toward, positive perspective, managing conflict, supporting life dreams, shared meaning) held up by two walls, trust and commitment. The practical point is that conflict skills sit on top of friendship, and rebuilding how you fight rarely works if the friendship underneath has eroded.

### Gottman or EFT: which is better?

Neither is universally better. Gottman-based work suits conflict-heavy couples who want tools and structure. EFT suits distant, withdrawn couples and attachment injuries, and has the stronger clinical-trial evidence. Most experienced therapists draw on both; the difference is which lens is primary. Choose by your problem and by the therapist, not by the brand.

### Does the Gottman Method work for couples who don't fight?

Less well than for couples who do. Its interventions target conflict behaviours and friendship habits. Couples whose problem is quiet emotional distance, where one partner has withdrawn and the other has stopped reaching, often find skills training addresses the wrong layer. EFT, which works on the cycle underneath, tends to serve these couples better.

### How long does Gottman couples therapy take?

Typically 10–20 sessions including the assessment phase, plus homework between sessions. Intensive "marathon" formats compress this into consecutive full days. Length depends on the assessment findings and how consistently the couple practises between sessions.
