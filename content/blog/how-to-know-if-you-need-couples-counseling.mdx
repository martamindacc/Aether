Most couples don't walk into their first session because something dramatic happened. More often, it's the accumulation of smaller things — the same argument on repeat, a slow drift into parallel lives, or a sense that you're no longer sure how to talk to each other about what matters.

## Six signs it might be time

1. **You have the same fight, just with different words.** If the topic changes but the pattern doesn't, that's usually not a communication problem you can out-argue — it's a structural one.
2. **One or both of you has stopped bringing things up.** Silence can look like peace. Often it's just conflict avoidance with a longer fuse.
3. **You're better at parenting, work, or friendships together than you are at being partners.** The relationship works everywhere except where it's supposed to matter most.
4. **Repair takes longer than it used to.** Every couple fights. What changes is how quickly you find your way back to each other afterward.
5. **You're keeping score.** When the relationship starts to feel transactional, it's a sign that trust or fairness needs active repair.
6. **You've started rehearsing the conversation with other people instead of your partner.** If friends, family, or a group chat know more about what's wrong than your partner does, that's worth naming out loud.

## Why an outside perspective helps

A therapist isn't there to referee who's right. The value of a third party is structural: someone who can slow the conversation down, name the pattern underneath the argument, and hold both people accountable to the same standard at the same time. Couples often find that what took months to talk around gets named clearly in a single session.

If any of this sounds familiar, it doesn't mean the relationship is in trouble — it usually means you're both ready for a different kind of conversation.
